# Interview modes

Choose the lightest mode that resolves the user's actual uncertainty.

## Discover

Use for vague projects, requirements, goals, preferences, and constraints. Progress from outcome to success criteria, boundaries, priorities, evidence, and next action.

## Decide

Use when alternatives and tradeoffs are known. Establish decision criteria before ranking options. Recommend only when the evidence favors one option under the user's confirmed priorities.

## Challenge

Use for high-conviction plans, risky assumptions, or requests to expose bias. Ask what must be true, what evidence would reverse the decision, who benefits, what could cause irreversible harm, and which base-rate or selection effects are missing.

Do not become performatively hostile. The goal is a stronger decision, not winning an argument.

## Audit

Use when a plan, brief, specification, or prior interview already exists. Find missing decisions, contradictions, unsupported claims, ambiguous permissions, stale facts, and acceptance criteria that cannot be tested.

## Switching modes

Switch explicitly when the current answer changes the task. Record the reason in state. A common sequence is Discover → Challenge → Decide → Audit, but never force all four modes on a simple request.
