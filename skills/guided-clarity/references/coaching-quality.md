# Coaching-quality questions

Use this rubric for goals, beliefs, tradeoffs, risk, commitment, and consequential decisions. “Coaching” here means helping the user think more clearly and retain agency; it does not imply therapy, diagnosis, credentialed coaching, or guaranteed outcomes.

## Six gates

A strong question is:

1. **Contextual** — builds on the user's confirmed words and does not make them repeat context.
2. **Generative** — reveals a criterion, assumption, tension, option, or consequence that can change the decision.
3. **Neutral** — does not hide the preferred answer in the wording. Put any recommendation in the explicit recommendation field.
4. **Consequential** — explains what the answer unlocks or prevents.
5. **Answerable** — asks one cognitive task, uses the correct control, and offers a correction path.
6. **Actionable** — the answer changes state, a decision, an experiment, a boundary, or the next evidence request.

Fail and rewrite a question when any gate is missing.

## Adaptive lenses

Choose one lens—the one with highest information gain:

- **Outcome:** What observable result matters, and what would count as enough?
- **Reality:** What is confirmed, measured, assumed, or still unknown?
- **Assumption:** What must be true, and what evidence would falsify it?
- **Tension:** Which two desired outcomes conflict, and what rule resolves the conflict?
- **Options:** Which compatible components or exclusive paths are genuinely available?
- **Risk:** What loss, reversibility, control, or second-order effect is hidden?
- **Commitment:** What decision, experiment, owner, or next checkpoint follows?

Do not march through every lens as a script. Select the next lens from state.

## Anti-patterns

Reject:

- questions whose answer is already known;
- stacked questions that require several unrelated decisions;
- false dichotomies and incomplete option sets;
- “don't you think…” or other leading constructions;
- vague aspiration without observable criteria;
- recommendation hidden inside the question;
- arbitrary selection caps;
- shame, pressure, flattery, gamification, or manufactured urgency;
- pseudo-therapeutic interpretation or claims about unconscious motives.

## Quick score

Score each gate 0 or 1. Require 6/6 for a consequential question. Store the score only in internal evaluation or test artifacts; do not clutter the active interview card with it.
